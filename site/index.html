Finance incidents

Banks, insurers, payments, fintech and crypto

Not attacks: the JPMorgan Chase and wider US bank outages of 16 November were a software bug, per the bank and US officials. Krungthai Bank's email outage from 12 November followed the Microsoft 365 outage.

How financial firms were hit

1 · Vendors

Through a supplier

Arkéa, Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan's providers, Sawyer Savings Bank, SMBC, HSBC (Optimizely) and AU Small Finance Bank were all reached through a third party.

2 · Speed

AI sweeps a sector

One campaign breached 16 South Korean lenders. The FBI says ARTEX intrusions reached privileged access in under three hours.

3 · Cash-out

Wire, stablecoin, gone

Arkéa's money went to exchanges, then stablecoins. About $30m was laundered before the bank disclosed the theft.

4 · Deception

Deepfake calls

North Korea's FaceStealer scheme used fake video calls, some with deepfakes, to steal more than $400m in crypto.

What banks should do now

  1. Lock down provider access

    Phishing-resistant sign-in, named accounts and a kill switch on every provider link to payment, core or document systems.

    Arkéa, Visa, Infoblox

  2. Guard the bank's own accounts

    Velocity limits and two-person release on outbound treasury transfers, especially to exchanges.

    Arkéa

  3. Pre-arrange crypto freezes

    Tested contacts at exchanges and stablecoin issuers, used in the first hour.

    Arkéa, Binance

  4. Audit third-party web scripts

    Inventory every script on banking pages and enforce a content security policy.

    Optimizely / HSBC

  5. Verify video and voice

    No payment or access change on the strength of a call or video alone.

    FaceStealer

  6. Patch Outlook and Puppet

    November Office update everywhere, block outbound NTLM, and patch Puppet Server before 1 December.

    CVE-2026-124194, CVE-2026-115731

What the nightly job reads

Every source below is free and public. Government and standards bodies come first, then established security data services, then a short list of security newsrooms for who-was-hit reporting. Each card shows the exact address the collector calls and what it feeds on this page.

Tier A

Official or primary

Governments, national CERTs, standards bodies, regulator filings, vendors on their own products. Can confirm an incident or an exploited flaw on its own.

Tier B

Established data services

Non-profits and major research teams with a track record. Strong context; an incident needs a second source or the victim's word.

Tier C

Reporting and claims

Security press and leak-site trackers. Shows as "Reported" or "Claim" until a Tier A source or the victim confirms.
